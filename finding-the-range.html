6.1 Data Range

How spread out is your data? The range tells you — using just one subtraction. Find the biggest value, find the smallest, and take one from the other.

Big idea 1

What is the range?

How spread out is the data?

The range measures spread — the gap between the biggest and smallest values in a data set.

Range = Maximum − Minimum. Find the highest value, find the lowest value, then subtract.

A bigger range = more spread out. A smaller range = more consistent.

Set it out in three steps

  1. Find the maximum (highest) value.
  2. Find the minimum (lowest) value.
  3. Subtract: maximum − minimum. Keep the units in your answer!
TeacherThe range is a measure of spread, not a typical value. Watch for two traps: doing min − max (a negative answer) and dropping the units.

How to work it out, step by step

Same data, written the way you should set it out in your book. Click “Next step”.

1 Find the  MAXIMUM  (highest) value 15
2 Find the  MINIMUM  (lowest) value 2
3 Subtract: Maximum − Minimum 15 − 2 = 13
✓ The range is 13 books
TeacherModel the layout aloud: “Maximum 15, minimum 2, so 15 − 2 = 13 books.” Insist the units appear in the final line.

🟢 Green Find the range Must complete
  1. Find the range: 3, 8, 1, 6, 4
  2. A student’s daily screen time (minutes): 45, 90, 30, 75, 60. What is the range?
  3. Quiz scores: 12, 7, 18, 5, 14. What is the range?
  4. Morning temperatures: 9°C, 14°C, 6°C, 11°C, 16°C. Find the range.
  5. Fish sold each day: 22, 35, 17, 41, 28. What is the range?
  6. Find the range: 100, 250, 175, 300, 125
✓ Answers
  1. 8 − 1 = 7
  2. 90 − 30 = 60 minutes
  3. 18 − 5 = 13 points
  4. 16°C − 6°C = 10°C
  5. 41 − 17 = 24 fish
  6. 300 − 100 = 200
TeacherCheck students scanned for the true max/min (data is unordered) and kept units on Q2 and Q4.
🟠 Amber Find the range and explain Extend
  1. Minutes reading each night: 20, 45, 10, 55, 30, 40, 15. Find the range and write one sentence about what it tells you.
  2. Sprint times (seconds) — Group A: 14, 16, 13, 15, 17; Group B: 11, 19, 14, 21, 10. Find both ranges. Which group was more consistent?
  3. Library visitors over 7 days: 112, 98, 143, 87, 156, 102, 119. Find the range and explain what it tells you.
  4. Tomatoes picked each week: 15, 23, 8, 31, 19, 27. Find the range. Does it tell you how many she usually picks?
✓ Answers & guidance
  1. 55 − 10 = 45 minutes. Very spread out — some read 45 minutes more per night than others.
  2. Group A: 17 − 13 = 4 s. Group B: 21 − 10 = 11 s. Group A is more consistent (smaller range).
  3. 156 − 87 = 69 people. Numbers varied a lot day to day — busy days had up to 69 more visitors.
  4. 31 − 8 = 23 tomatoes. No — the range only shows the spread, not the typical amount.
TeacherQ4 is the key discussion: range describes spread, not the typical (average) value. Push for that distinction.
🔴 Red Putting it in context Challenge
  1. A data set has a range of 24. The minimum is 13. What is the maximum? Explain how you worked it out.
  2. A coach checks if her team is becoming more consistent.
    Week 1: 45, 67, 52, 71, 49 · Week 2: 55, 62, 58, 68, 53 · Week 3: 59, 64, 61, 66, 60. Find each range and explain the trend.
✓ Answers & guidance
  1. Maximum = minimum + range = 13 + 24 = 37. Rearrange range = max − min into max = min + range.
  2. Week 1: 71 − 45 = 26. Week 2: 68 − 53 = 15. Week 3: 66 − 59 = 7. The range shrinks each week, so the team is becoming more consistent.
TeacherQ1 tests rearranging the formula. Q2 links a shrinking range to improving consistency over time.
