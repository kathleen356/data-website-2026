Big idea 1

What is a pictograph?

A pictograph is a way of showing data using images. Each image stands for a certain number of things.

The key — one picture can mean many!

In a pictograph, one picture does not always mean just one thing. A little note called the key tells you how many things one picture is worth.

1 picture
really means
10 real apples

So to find the total you don't count the pictures — you multiply the number of pictures by the key. Try it: add or take away apple pictures and watch the total change.

And a half picture is worth half the key. If one apple = 10, then half an apple = 5.

TeacherName the misconception. Many students count the pictures (e.g. "3 apples") instead of using the key ("3 × 10 = 30"). Say the key out loud together before every question, and point to it each time the graph changes.

TeacherMake the key matter. Set every stall, then change only the key slider. Ask: "the pictures didn't move — why did the total change?" This is the core idea of a scaled pictograph.
Putting knowledge in context

Where do we see this in real life?

Pictographs and frequency tables aren't just classroom exercises. Shops use frequency tables to plan staff rosters, and news reports use pictographs to make big numbers easy to understand at a glance. Whenever someone needs to turn a pile of data into a quick, clear picture, one of these tools is usually behind it.
